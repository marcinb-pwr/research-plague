# Sources, scope and provenance

## Primary work

**Gastaldi, Girolamo.** *Tractatus de avertenda et profliganda peste politico-legalis*. Bologna: Manolessi, 1684.

- [Google Books edition initially supplied for the research](https://books.google.com/books?id=dPUHQlrUZWEC)

The website's local extracts come from Internet Archive item **`bub_gb_7JW_fmB33XEC`**, a Google-digitized copy. The manifest records the downloaded PDF's SHA-256 hash, precise scan pages and printed-page labels. The linked Google Books record with ID `dPUHQlrUZWEC` is a different digitized copy of the same edition; its viewer page numbers must not be substituted for the excerpt source's scan numbers.

## What is counted

The edition has **261 chapters** and main-text pagination through **792**. The source PDF has **997 scan pages**, including preliminary matter, plates and end matter.

| Chapters | Function in the book | Treatment in this wiki |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I–XIII | Narrative and account of the response | Context and supplementary measures |
| XIV–XL | Facilities, plans and physical arrangements | Infrastructure context and selected illustration |
| XLI | Introduction to the documentary collection | Explains preservation and arrangement of edicts/instructions |
| XLII–CCXXVII | 186 chapters reproducing orders and edicts | Included in the operational catalogue |
| CCXXVIII–CCXLV | 18 operational instruction chapters | Included in the operational catalogue |
| CCXLVI–CCLXI | General, philosophical and medical discussion and counsel | Separate supplementary material; not automatically Roman enactments |

The **204 operational chapters** are the unit of documentary coverage. They are not 204 independent intervention technologies, nor a verified list of “256 NPIs.” A chapter may contain several provisions; another may renew, extend or rescind an earlier one. The final edict in chapter CCXXVII is dated **1 March 1658**, so the collected documents extend beyond calendar 1657.

The website's **13 groups** are a modern analytical classification, not section headings used by Gastaldi. Counts assigned to a primary group describe filing choices. Chapters can concern more than one group.

## Catalogue provenance

The starting catalogue was the research workbook **`Gastaldi_1684_interventions.xlsx`** prepared from the original volume. Its chapter summaries were checked against the operative bodies through the scan/OCR, with image checks for illegible or ambiguous sections. The website retains the printed-page locators, analytical summaries, expanded provisions and verification notes.

The catalogue is an English research aid. Its summaries condense the documents, sometimes including recurring legal formulae. Original-language transcriptions and English working translations are now provided as separately versioned chapter editions where available. A summary's existence does not imply that a full-text edition is available or reviewed. The workbook remains the initial catalogue snapshot; later corrections are recorded in the repository's catalogue and chapter editions.

## Original text and English translation

The first complete reading editions cover chapters LV (55) and XCIX (99). Each pairs original-language passages with English translations and exact facsimile pages, including headings, operative provisions, dates and signatures within the chapter boundary. Source-page content belonging to adjoining chapters is excluded from the transcription and identified in the boundary note; the facsimile retains the complete printed pages.

These are the texts **as printed in Gastaldi's 1684 edition**, which reproduces earlier documents. The scan does not by itself authenticate a separately surviving 1656 broadside. The document's date, the edition's publication year and the digital edition's revision date are recorded separately.

The reading transcription normalizes long s, joins words divided across printed lines, and regularizes printer spacing and apostrophes. Historical spelling is retained. Each edition specifies its policy, exceptions and any uncertain readings. Editorial passage labels are distinguished from paragraph numbers in the source. Translation notes explain terms, abbreviations and legal qualifications without silently adding contextual claims to the translated text.

Preparation and review are attributed explicitly. The initial editions contain AI-prepared transcriptions compared with the source images and AI-prepared English working translations. Human specialist review is pending. Automated validation checks coverage declarations, identifiers, paired fields and source links; it does not establish philological accuracy. A second AI pass is recorded as an AI review, not independent human scholarship.

For chapter 99, the edict dated 24 August 1656 expressly recalls the proclamation of 20 June 1656 in chapter 55. The earlier document supplies the immediate-reporting requirement and reporting details that the later edict does not repeat in full. This explicit documentary relationship is displayed separately from thematic related-entry suggestions.

Edition JSON files retain stable passage identifiers, notes, the original PDF hash, coverage scope, revision events and separate transcription/translation review states. Cite a translation using its chapter, passage and edition version, alongside Gastaldi's printed page. See [docs/EDITIONS.md](docs/EDITIONS.md) for the data contract and review workflow.

## Source display

Selected excerpts pair downloadable PDF pages with local page images. Selection is illustrative rather than a claim that every chapter has a local facsimile. Chapters without a selected extract link to the full scan.

A printed page such as **417** is a label on the historical page. A scan page is that page's one-based position in the digitized PDF. Preliminary pages, illustrations and duplicated pagination make a single constant offset unreliable.

Descriptions of excerpts are analyst paraphrases, stored as `readingGuide`; they are distinct from the aligned translations in chapter editions. Original punctuation, spelling and typography may differ from modern Italian. OCR can confuse letters, numbers and names and should not override the image.

## Interpretation

- **Prescribed policy:** an edict establishes what was ordered, not that it was uniformly followed.
- **Reported practice:** Gastaldi's narrative can describe arrangements and claimed outcomes, but requires contextual reading.
- **General counsel:** recommendations in later chapters can draw on other places, times and medical theories.
- **Modern analysis:** grouping and discussion of likely purposes are interpretive aids rather than causal estimates.

Some regulations discriminate by religion, ethnicity, status or poverty and threaten severe punishment. Those features are part of the evidence. Neither their inclusion nor their historical explanation is an endorsement.

Contemporary theories of air, humors, contagion and divine assistance coexist in the work. Modern biological assessment must be separate from Gastaldi's proposed mechanisms. Clinical and ritual remedies are identified as context and are not counted as public-health NPIs.
